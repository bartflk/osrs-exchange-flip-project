Your specific questions
Is there an indicator for buying during work hours and selling during evenings?

Potentially yes, but it's something to measure rather than assume. If you collect timestamped price and margin data, you can build:

Hour-of-day average return after buying.
Hour-of-day average selling premium.
A "Time-of-Day Edge" score showing whether certain hours consistently outperform. Different items will likely have different patterns, and the effect may vary by region and day of the week.
Same for weekends?

Absolutely. Many items have recurring weekly behavior. Useful metrics include:

Weekend premium (% difference from weekday average).
Day-of-week seasonality.
Heatmaps showing average price or margin by hour and weekday.
Is there a bot detection system on items?

There is no public API that identifies botted items, so any "bot detection" would be heuristic, not definitive. Strong signals include:

Sustained high supply with steadily declining prices.
Volume increases without matching player-count increases.
Little or no daily or weekly seasonality despite human activity cycles.
Consistent undercutting and rapid replenishment after sales.

Think of it as a Bot Pressure Score rather than "this item is botted."

What do bots affect?

Bots primarily influence supply, especially for:

Gathered resources (ores, logs, fish).
Boss drops from heavily botted bosses.
Common skilling materials.

Their effects often include:

Lower long-term prices.
Higher trading volume.
Tighter margins due to competition.
Faster recovery after demand spikes because supply is replenished quickly.

Assigning an exact percentage isn't realistic without privileged data; the impact differs greatly between items and over time.

Correlation between player count and the market

This is one of the strongest features you could build. Compare hourly or daily player counts against:

Price changes.
Trading volume.
Spread.
Margin.
Volatility.

You'll likely find that some items are highly correlated with player activity (consumables, PvM supplies), while others are driven more by updates or speculation.
